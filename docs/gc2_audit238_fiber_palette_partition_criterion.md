# GC-II Audit 238 — Exact fiber-palette partition criterion

Status: **PROVED (structural criterion); IMPORTED/KNOWN mechanism boundary; GC-specific accounting consequence OPEN**

## Setup
Let `W` be a finite world set, `p:W->P` the visible projection, `g:W->D` the required global decision, `M` a finite message/action alphabet, and `L(w) subseteq M` the admissible messages at world `w`.

An exact admissible translator is a map `m:W->M` with `m(w) in L(w)` such that a decoder `delta:P x M -> D` satisfies

`delta(p(w),m(w)) = g(w)` for every `w`.

Audits 236–237 showed that this is list coloring on the decision-conflict graph and that cardinal/marginal summaries of admissibility are incomplete.

## Theorem 238.1 — Fiber-palette partition criterion
An exact admissible translator exists **iff**, for every projection value `z in p(W)`, there are pairwise-disjoint message palettes

`C_{z,d} subseteq M`,  `d in g(p^{-1}(z))`,

such that

`L(w) intersect C_{z,g(w)} != empty`

for every `w` with `p(w)=z`.

Equivalently, for each fiber there is a labeling

`lambda_z:M -> g(p^{-1}(z)) union {unused}`

such that every world `w` in that fiber has at least one admissible message `a in L(w)` with `lambda_z(a)=g(w)`.

### Proof
If a translator/decoder exists, put `C_{z,d}={a in M: delta(z,a)=d}`. Decoder functionality makes these palettes pairwise disjoint, and the translator choice for each world witnesses intersection with its required palette.

Conversely, choose for each world any admissible message in `L(w) intersect C_{z,g(w)}` and decode every message in `C_{z,d}` as `d`. Pairwise disjointness makes the decoder well-defined. QED.

## Corollary 238.2 — Fibers decouple
Because the decoder also observes `z=p(w)`, the same message may be assigned different decisions in different fibers. Hence global feasibility is exactly the conjunction of independent fiber feasibility problems.

This sharply localizes the coupling obstruction found in Audit 237: only incidence alignment **within decision-incompatible projection fibers** matters in this deterministic one-way model.

## Corollary 238.3 — Binary-decision signed hitting criterion
For a fiber containing decisions `0,1`, exact translation exists iff there is a subset `S subseteq M` such that

- every list belonging to a decision-0 world intersects `S`, and
- every list belonging to a decision-1 world intersects `M\S`.

Thus feasibility is a signed set-system coloring/hitting problem on the message alphabet, not a function of row/column degree marginals.

## Exact SAT formulation
Introduce Boolean variables `x[z,a,d]` meaning message `a` is assigned to decision `d` in fiber `z`.

Constraints:

1. exclusivity: for each `(z,a)`, at most one `d` has `x[z,a,d]=1`;
2. coverage: for each world `w`, `OR_{a in L(w)} x[p(w),a,g(w)]`.

These constraints are necessary and sufficient by Theorem 238.1. This is a finite exact operational criterion, not a heuristic score.

## Accounting consequence
A capability-accounting state for this model can be compressed from the world-level conflict graph to the family of **decision-labeled list hypergraphs per projection fiber**. Audit 237 already proves that ordinary degree marginals lose essential information. Theorem 238.1 identifies exactly what information is sufficient: the signed/labeled incidence hypergraph up to message relabeling within each fiber.

This does **not** establish a novel general combinatorial theorem. It is a structured reformulation of list-coloring/CSP feasibility for the special complete-multipartite conflict graphs generated by `(p,g)`. Hall-type conditions are generally only necessary for arbitrary list coloring, not sufficient, so no Hall-only completeness claim is made.

## Edge/degenerate checks
- Empty world set: feasible vacuously.
- A world with `L(w)=empty`: infeasible.
- One decision in a fiber: feasible iff every world in that fiber has a nonempty list; choose the union of all usable messages as its palette.
- Injective projection: every fiber has one world; feasibility iff all lists are nonempty.
- Unrestricted lists `L(w)=M`, `M!=empty`: every fiber is feasible iff the number of decisions in the fiber is at most `|M|`; this reduces to Audit 235.
- Message names are irrelevant: simultaneous permutation of `M` and all lists preserves feasibility.
- Adding admissible incidences cannot destroy feasibility; deleting incidences cannot create it.
- Adding a decision-incompatible world can destroy feasibility; deleting worlds cannot destroy it.

## Prior-art collision boundary
List coloring is established graph theory, and Hall-style conditions for list coloring are known not to be sufficient in general. The present result should therefore be used as an exact GC operational normal form and as a launch point for budgeted *generation/editing* of admissibility incidence, not claimed as invention of list coloring, SAT, hitting sets, or hypergraph coloring.
