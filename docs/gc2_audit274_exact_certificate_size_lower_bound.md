# GC-II Audit 274 — Exact convertibility certificates can require exponential size

## Scope
Finite typed budget semantics from Audits 265–273. Lower cost is better. For a finite attainable set `Y subseteq {0,1}^d`, write `P(Y)` for its Pareto-minimal vectors and `Up(Y)={q: exists y in Y with y <= q}` for the feasible-budget upset.

This audit attacks program item (6): whether exact typed convertibility admits a universally compact finite certificate.

## Theorem 274.1 — irredundancy of the Pareto kernel
**Status: PROVED.**

Let `A` be a finite antichain under coordinatewise order. In any exact representation of `Up(A)` as a union of principal upsets generated by attainable vectors, every `a in A` is indispensable.

### Proof
At budget `q=a`, feasibility holds because `a<=q`. If a different generator `a' in A` also certified q, then `a'<=a`, contradicting antichain incomparability. Hence deleting `a` changes the answer at its private witness budget `q=a`. Therefore all members are required. QED.

Equivalently, the minimal generators of a finite upset are unique and are exactly its Pareto-minimal elements.

## Theorem 274.2 — exponential exact-certificate lower bound
**Status: PROVED.**

For every dimension `d`, there exists a finite binary typed-budget system whose exact Pareto-kernel certificate requires

`M_d = binom(d, floor(d/2))`

distinct generators.

Take the middle layer

`A_d={x in {0,1}^d : sum_i x_i=floor(d/2)}`.

All members have equal Hamming weight, so no distinct pair is coordinatewise comparable. Thus `A_d` is an antichain and Theorem 274.1 forces every member to appear in any exact principal-upset generator certificate. Its cardinality is the middle binomial coefficient.

By Stirling asymptotics,

`M_d = Theta(2^d/sqrt(d))`.

Therefore exact finite typed convertibility is complete via the Pareto kernel, but that kernel has no polynomial-size worst-case guarantee as dimension grows.

## Corollary 274.3 — every omitted frontier point has an exact adversarial query
**Status: PROVED.**

For the construction above, omitting any frontier vector `a` is detected by the single budget query `q=a`. Hence the lower bound is semantic, not an artifact of a particular enumeration algorithm.

## Relation to Omega_G
If an approximation is allowed, Audit 273 already connects multiplicative approximation to Omega. The present theorem concerns exact (`Omega=1`) feasibility/convertibility certificates. It explains structurally why approximate Pareto certificates can be much smaller while exact capability accounting may be exponentially large.

## Edge and invariance checks
- `d=1`: one generator; theorem remains valid.
- Empty attainable set: empty upset, zero generators; excluded from the nonempty middle-layer construction.
- Dominated vectors never help reduce the minimal generator set; only Pareto-minimal vectors matter.
- Coordinate permutations preserve the construction and certificate size.
- Positive coordinate rescaling embeds the same order-theoretic lower bound into non-binary typed costs.
- The statement is about explicit principal-upset / attainable-witness certificates. It does **not** rule out succinct symbolic descriptions, circuits, or problem-specific implicit representations; claiming such a universal lower bound would require a separate complexity model.

## Exact verification
`experiments/audit274_certificate_lower_bound.py` constructs the middle layer for `d=1..12`, verifies pairwise incomparability, verifies that each vector is the unique frontier witness feasible at its own budget, and checks exact cardinality against `binom(d,floor(d/2))`.

## Prior-art collision
**Combinatorial mechanism: IMPORTED/KNOWN.** Sperner's theorem states that the largest antichain of the Boolean lattice has size `binom(d,floor(d/2))`; exponential Pareto fronts are also well known in multiobjective shortest-path/discrete optimization. Therefore neither the middle-layer antichain nor exponential Pareto-front size is claimed as new mathematics.

The GC-II value is narrower and operational: combined with Audits 265 and 267, it proves that the exact complete typed convertibility kernel can be intrinsically exponentially large and that each required frontier element has a concrete budget query witnessing its indispensability. This identifies a sharp exactness-versus-compression boundary for capability accounting.
